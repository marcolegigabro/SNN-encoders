# Figures

All figures of task-2-simplified: lossy compression of a point process with a
learned codec (SNN or GRU encoder -> discrete latent -> entropy coding -> decoder),
compared with Rubin's (1974) binned-count scheme and his rate-distortion bounds.

Shared conventions, unless a figure says otherwise:

| Item | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Source | homogeneous Poisson process, rate lambda, window T = 1 |
| Main operating regime | lambda T = 5 (about 5 events per window) |
| Distortion D | Rubin's counting-function L1, (1/T) * integral of \|N(t) - N_hat(t)\| dt, computed exactly on the decoded events |
| Rate | size of the real range-coded bitstream (constriction), in bits per unit time (= bits per window here) |
| Evaluation set | fixed 10,000-window test set (`sources.eval_set`), the first 5,000 for the interpretability figures |
| Learned codecs | one seed per point; each point is the final checkpoint (`last.pt`) of one beta run |
| Palette | dataviz reference palette; categorical slots always in the same order (blue, orange, aqua) |

> **Status (2026-09-15).** Every learned-codec figure below was produced with the
> first training setup: decoder reading raw level values (width 256), SNN recurrent
> gradient detached, 10,000 steps per run. Diagnostics in `runs/diag/` showed that the
> decoder, the SNN's short gradient memory and the training length limit the
> high-rate end. The four sweeps are being re-run once with the fixes (32-d level
> embeddings, decoder width 1024, SNN leak 0.97 at init with the recurrent gradient
> scaled by 0.5, 20,000 steps) into `runs/sweep2-*`, and these figures will be
> regenerated from them. The baseline and theory figure is final. Numbers quoted here
> are from the current figures.

## `overview.png`

A one-page summary of the whole task, in reading order, made for presenting the
project. Panels:

1. **Problem and distortion:** a real test window with 5 events, its reconstruction by the baseline (2 bins), and the shaded area between the two counting functions, which is D.
2. **Theory and baseline:** Rubin's bounds (gray band) and the binned-count codec curve at lambda T = 5, with the three steps of the algorithm.
3. **Codec architecture:** every block from binning to decoded events, with layer sizes and formulas. The box outline says whether a block is learned by gradient (blue), estimated from symbol counts (orange: the prior), or a fixed operation (gray).
4. **Training:** the rate warm-up schedule (rate weight vs training step) and the training settings.
5. **Results:** distortion of the SNN and GRU codecs relative to the baseline at the same rate (first round, 8x16), with the GRU re-run numbers in the note.
6. **Interpretability:** for the SNN 8x16 codec at beta 200, how strongly the best latent coordinate tracks each window feature.

Panels 5 and 6 use first-round runs and will be updated from the re-run.

Generated by `scripts/overview.py`.

## Rate-distortion figures

### 1. `rd-theory-baseline.png`

**Question:** does the non-neural baseline behave as Rubin's theory predicts?

Three panels, one per source rate (lambda T = 2, 5, 10). In each panel:

- **x-axis:** distortion D (log scale). **y-axis:** rate in bits per unit time.
- **Gray band:** Rubin's bounds on the rate-distortion function, lambda ln(1/2D) <= R(D) <= lambda ln(e/4D) (eq. 40). They hold for T -> infinity. A real codec can sit anywhere above the lower edge; points above the band are normal, points below it would indicate a bug.
- **Blue line:** Rubin's Theorem 5, the analytic curve of the binned-count scheme (D = lambda dT / 4, R = H_Poisson(lambda dT) / dT).
- **Blue dots:** the same scheme simulated on the test set, events placed at the bin midpoint.
- **Orange dots:** the same bits, events placed at the medians of the order statistics inside each bin (Theorem 4).
- **Horizontal line:** the count cost H(N)/T, the rate needed just to send the number of events of a window (2.5, 3.2 and 3.7 bits).
- **Black diamond:** the best fixed reconstruction, i.e. the distortion reachable with 0 bits.

**How to read it:** the blue dots sit on the blue line at every bin width, and the
rates are real bitstream sizes (within about 0.1% of the ideal -log2 p), so the
baseline implementation matches the theory. All points lie above the band. The
orange dots show that smarter placement is free distortion reduction when bins are
wide: at lambda T = 10 with one bin, D drops from 2.50 to 0.95 for the same 3.7 bits.
At fine bins both placements coincide.

Generated by `scripts/baseline.py` (data in `runs/baseline/baseline.json`).

### 2. `rd-lambda5.png`

**Question:** how do the learned codecs compare with the baseline?

Source lambda T = 5, latent Z in {0..15}^8 (8x16, the spec's example), scalar
ordinal quantizer, beta swept from 5 to 2000.

- **Left panel, rate vs distortion:** same axes and gray band as figure 1. Blue: the baseline with median placement, joined into a curve, starting at the 0-bit point (black diamond). Orange: learned codec with the SNN encoder. Aqua: learned codec with the parameter-matched GRU encoder (9.4k vs 9.3k parameters).
- **Right panel, distortion ratio:** D of the learned codec divided by the baseline's D at the same rate (log scale). Between two baseline points the baseline is interpolated linearly in (rate, D), which is achievable by time-sharing the two schemes across windows, so a ratio below 1 is a genuine win.

**How to read it:** below about 6 bits both learned codecs beat the baseline
(ratio 0.70x at about 2 bits; 0.90x SNN and 0.77x GRU at about 4.5 bits). Around 8.5
bits they tie (SNN 1.16x, GRU 1.02x). Above that the learned curves turn vertical and
flatten out (SNN near D = 0.13, GRU near D = 0.09) while the baseline keeps
improving, so the ratio climbs to 11x (SNN) and 7x (GRU) at about 29 bits. The GRU is
at least as good as the SNN at every rate. At beta = 5 the SNN collapsed to 0 bits;
that point sits on the black diamond and is left out of the right panel.

Generated by `scripts/visualize.py` from `runs/sweep-snn-l8a16-lt5` and
`runs/sweep-ann-l8a16-lt5`.

### 4. `rd-lambda5-snn-latents.png` and `rd-lambda5-gru-latents.png`

**Question:** does a larger alphabet (8x64) fix the high-rate end?

Same layout as figure 2, one figure per encoder. Orange: 8x16 latent (beta 5 to
2000). Aqua: 8x64 latent (beta 50 to 10000). Up to 32 and 48 bits per window.

**How to read it:** at low and mid rates the two latents give the same curve. At
the high-rate end the 8x64 codec spends 10 to 14 more bits for a small gain: best D
goes from 0.134 to 0.126 (SNN) and from 0.093 to 0.084 (GRU). The ratio panel climbs
to about 32x (SNN) and 17x (GRU) because the baseline keeps improving at those rates.

What this figure led to: a hand-built code in the same 8x64 space reaches D = 0.038,
so the latent size was not the limit. Follow-up diagnostics (see `runs/diag/`) found
that the decoder, then the encoders themselves, cap the learned codecs; hence the
planned re-run.

Generated by `scripts/visualize.py` with the 8x16 and 8x64 sweeps of each encoder.

## Interpretability figures

Produced by `scripts/analyze.py` for each sweep (numbers saved in each sweep's
`analysis.json`). Files named `*-snn-sweep-snn-l8a16-lt5.png` belong to the SNN
encoder with the 8x16 latent, `*-gru-sweep-ann-l8a16-lt5.png` to the GRU; the same
figures for the 8x64 latent are in `l8a64/`.

Each figure picks three operating points among the runs that did not collapse to 0
bits: the lowest rate, the median, and the highest. Their titles give beta, rate and D.

| Sweep | Low | Mid | High |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SNN 8x16 | beta 10 (2.1 bits) | beta 200 (17.4 bits) | beta 2000 (29.5 bits) |
| GRU 8x16 | beta 5 (1.5 bits) | beta 100 (14.2 bits) | beta 2000 (29.1 bits) |
| SNN 8x64 | beta 50 (8.8 bits) | beta 1000 (39.2 bits) | beta 10000 (43.9 bits) |
| GRU 8x64 | beta 50 (8.3 bits) | beta 1000 (37.7 bits) | beta 10000 (42.3 bits) |

Window features used throughout (`src/features.py`): event count N; count in the
first and in the second half of the window; first, last and mean event time; longest
gap (window edges included); coefficient of variation of the inter-event intervals
(ISI CV, a burstiness measure, defined for windows with at least 3 events).

Coordinate numbers (z0 to z7) are arbitrary: the same role can land on z3 in one run
and z7 in another. Compare roles, not indices, across runs.

### 5. `interp-correlations-*.png`

**Question:** what does each latent coordinate encode?

Three heatmaps, one per operating point. Rows: the 8 coordinates. Columns: the
window features. Color: Spearman rank correlation between the coordinate's symbol and
the feature, from blue (-1) through gray (0) to red (+1). Values with |rho| >= 0.5
are printed. Gray rows marked "unused (constant)" are coordinates the codec switched
off to save bits (symbol entropy below 0.05 bits).

**How to read it (8x16):**

- At the lowest rate a single coordinate is active, and it is an event counter weighted toward the start of the window (SNN beta 10: |rho| 0.86 with N, 0.84 with the first-half count, only 0.36 with the second-half count). The metric explains the bias: an event wrongly placed or missed at time t costs up to T - t, so early events matter more.
- At mid rate a second strong role appears: the time of the first event (SNN beta 200: z7, rho +0.98), next to a count coordinate (z3, +0.87 with N).
- At the highest rate count and timing information is spread over more coordinates. The last event time and the ISI CV never correlate strongly with any coordinate.

**8x64 (`l8a64/`):** the same roles appear (SNN beta 50: z0 tracks N at +0.92, z7
the first event at +0.94), with the information spread over more coordinates at high
rate.

### 6. `interp-reconstructions-*.png`

**Question:** what do decoded windows actually look like?

A grid: rows are three test windows with 2, 5 and 9 events; columns are the three
operating points. Each panel shows the original counting function N(t) (black step)
and the decoded one N_hat(t) (encoder color, orange for SNN, aqua for GRU). The ticks
below the curves mark the individual events: original (black, upper row of ticks)
and decoded (colored, lower row).

**How to read it (SNN 8x16):** at the lowest rate the decoder gets roughly the right
number of events with coarse timing (the 5-event window decodes to 4 events, the
9-event window to 8, both visibly shifted). As the rate grows the decoded events line
up with the true ones. Even at the highest rate small timing offsets remain and the
9-event window still decodes to 8 events: this residual error is the flat high-rate
end of the rate-distortion curves in figures 2 and 4.

### 7. `interp-usage-traversal-*.png`

**Question:** how is the code used, which coordinates matter, and what do they do to
the output? Drawn for the mid operating point only.

Four panels, left to right:

1. **Symbol usage:** heatmap of how often each coordinate (row) takes each symbol level (column), light to dark blue. The entropy of each coordinate is given next to its name, in bits (at most 4 bits for 16 levels, 6 bits for 64).
2. **Coordinate importance:** increase in D when one coordinate is replaced by its most frequent symbol for every window and the receiver decodes that altered code. A long bar means the decoder relies heavily on that coordinate.
3. and 4. **Traversals** of the two most important coordinates: all other coordinates are fixed at their median symbol, the traversed coordinate sweeps its used levels (light blue = low level, dark blue = high level), and each line is the decoded counting curve N_hat(t). The lowest and highest levels are labelled at the line ends.

**How to read it:**

- **SNN 8x16, beta 200:** importance is concentrated on two coordinates. Fixing z3 raises D by 0.70 and z7 by 0.33; all others add at most 0.12. The traversals make their roles visible: z3 scales the total count (the decoded curve ends between about 2.4 and 7.7 events), z7 slides the onset of the first event from t close to 0 to about 0.45.
- **SNN 8x64, beta 1000:** importance is spread over six coordinates (largest +0.33). Every coordinate uses a wide range of levels (3.9 to 5.4 bits of entropy). z1 again acts as a count (final value from about 1.4 to 6.8 events, the extra events added mostly late in the window) and z2 again moves the first event (onset from about t = 0 to 0.45).

### 8. `interp-features-vs-rate.png`

**Question:** does the kind of information captured change with the operating point,
and do the two encoders differ?

A 2x4 grid, one panel per window feature. x-axis: rate. y-axis: the strongest
|Spearman rho| between that feature and any active coordinate of the run. Orange:
SNN; aqua: GRU. One dot per non-collapsed beta run.

**How to read it (8x16):** the event count, and especially the first-half count, is
captured from the very first bits (|rho| about 0.8 to 0.9 at every rate). The
second-half count starts low (about 0.3 below 3 bits) and rises with rate. The first
event time is the next feature to be learned, earlier and more strongly by the SNN
(up to 0.98) than by the GRU (up to 0.85). The last event time stays below 0.5 and the
ISI CV below about 0.45 at every rate: late timing is cheap under this distortion, and
a Poisson source has no burst structure to encode. Burstiness becomes meaningful only
with the structured sources planned for Stage 6.

**8x64 (`l8a64/interp-features-vs-rate.png`):** the same ordering holds (counts first,
then first event time; last event and ISI CV weak), over rates from about 8 to 44 bits.

```bash
                            --sweep GRU=runs/sweep-ann-l8a16-lt5 \

                          --sweep GRU=runs/sweep-ann-l8a16-lt5     # figures 5 to 8

                          --sweep GRU=runs/sweep-ann-l8a64-lt5 --fig-dir figures/l8a64
```
